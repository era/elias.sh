Technology of the oppressed

2026-08-10

Most of the world depends today on technology from the USA. Europeans are starting to be concerned about this. The main reason is that for years they watched the USA bomb, start dictatorships, sanction, and kill around the globe, but never thought the USA would try the same thing on the continent.

Things changed: the USA became an openly Fascist country. What Obama, Bush and other Presidents did hidden from the world, Trump does in public. When Trump kidnapped the President of Venezuela, he didn't try to hide it saying the reason was for "freedom", but said openly it was because the oil. This just after spending months trying to get hold of Greenland. Trump is also supporting the Genocide of the Palestinian people, while bombing children in Iran. Nothing is hidden.

The USA is famous for applying technology sanctions on people who do not bend the knee. China, Iran and others were always targets of such acts. With the USA clearly spinning out of control, there is always the possibility of them sanctioning Europe as well. A small example of this was the brief sanction against foreigners using some AI tech. There is no guarantee Europe won't be sanctioned at some point, or at least some countries. Given that the USA is responsible for 70% of the whole cloud providers market, Europe is in a very fragile position.

Following the example of China, the EU is starting to invest and incentivise internal development of technology in order to build some Tech Sovereignty. The rest of the planet does not necessarily have the money or political power to do the same. If they dare to do it, the USA may bomb or sanction them before they are ready. Look at Brasil, the fact that the country has a payment system that is free is causing the USA to sanction the country, as "it's unfair to USA companies". So what can they do?

The truth is that we (we the people) have been doing this for years now, as we had no other choice. The book "Technology of the oppressed" by David Nemer lists actions that different communities in the Brasilian Favelas did in order to own tech: "David Nemer conducts an ethnographic study to analyze how favela residents appropriate everyday technologies, not only artifacts but spaces and processes, in order to alleviate oppressions in daily life. Nemer applies the concept of Mundane Technologies, which encompasses everyday, non-productive activities and desires that engage people. Embedded in the appropriation of these technologies are the processes of how people exercise their agency and consciousness, to mobilize towards a better quality of life, i.e. how they critically and consciously reinvent technology." This goes from fixing broken things, making a "gambiarra" in order to achieve a goal impossible with the original hardware/software to having spaces where people can teach each other.

These types of guerrilla actions are harder to become targets of sanctions and at the same time spread like fire. In Brasil we have o nucleo de tecnologia do MTST, part of the social movement of the workers without homes ("sem-teto") that focus on building the needed technology to support / fix workers' needs (e.g. sensors for vegetable gardens, tools to help to hire people in vulnerable situation) and emancipate people by teaching them how to build and not depend on the group. On a more global scale we have Thaura building and providing AI without Big Tech, and we also have Tech for Palestine supporting different projects to help the Palestinian People.

As it was said in the past: If we, the workers, are nothing, let us become everything.
