One of the coolest job I had was working as a Principal Software Engineer at Huawei (Irish R&D lab). There I was responsible for researching and implementing solutions for the observability problems they had on their Cloud systems. General-purpose systems and also open source solutions can handle small to large data volumes comfortably, but the greater scale, the more specialized systems tend to become. We needed to build systems to handle the telemetry of the whole Cloud business and all their data centers and regions. Very few companies in the world had a problem at this scale and all of them had their own private solution.

Our problems went from "how can we design a database to store this data while still ingesting and exposing close to real time data to all consumers", to "how fast can we alarm if something abnormal would happen". Huawei was also not afraid of spending a lot of money (on research and development) if it meant having a better system (cost and value wise). The USA media like to say that China companies copy the USA ones, but I can say that is just not true. Huawei was of course looking at the market and understanding trends and common industry solutions, but was also trying very hard to find its own answers. This combination of problem and allowance to try new ideas gave me the chance to build and learn a lot. I had to go much deeper on database designs, I went from designing the front-end of a database (the query engine and its language) to thinking on how we could leverage the workload to store the data into disk. This job made me feel like a real engineer. All of this having great mentors.

The main language we used at the lab was Go, C++ and Rust. Most of the times we were using software built inside (not very different from AWS) and I had less contact with open source while working there.

<img src="https://elias.sh/work/china_bus.jpeg" />

I also had the chance to visit different offices in China. It was an amazing trip. The Chinese are great hosts, I ate good food, visited places that looked like a Cyberpunk city, old and sacred places. All inside China was so cheap, so clean, so safe. I feel sometimes that what people say about Japan is true only in China: a modern and safe place that hasn't forgotten its origins.
